10.4 Simple factorisation rules

Coq extensible parsing is performed by Camlp4 which is essentially a LL1 parser. Hence, some care has to be made not to hide already existing rules by new rules. Some simple left factorisation work has to be done. Here is an example.

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p"     := (ex ? [x]p) (at level 10).

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p & q" := (ex2 ? [x]p [x]q) (at level 10).

In order to factorise the left part of the rules, the subexpression referred by p has to be at the same level in both rules. However the default behaviour puts p at the next existing level below 10 in the first rule (no associativity is the default), and at the level 10 in the second rule (level 10 is the default when not on the border of a notation). To fix this, we need to force the parsing level of p, as follows.

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p"     := (ex ? [x]p) (at level 10, p at level 9).

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p & q" := (ex2 ? [x]p [x]q)
Coq <   (at level 10, p at level 9).

Actually, for a better homogeneity between p and q, it is reasonable to put q at the same level as p, hence the final version of the rules

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p"     := (ex ? [x]p) (at level 10, p at level 9).

Coq < Notation "'EX' x | p & q" := (ex2 ? [x]p [x]q)
Coq <   (at level 10, p, q at level 9).

For the sake of factorisation with Coq predefined rules, simple rules have to be observed for notations starting with a symbol: e.g. rules starting with ``{'' or ``<'' should be put at level 1 and rules starting with ``('' at level 0. The list of Coq predefined notations are given on figure 10.2.
